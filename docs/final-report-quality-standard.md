# Resourceful Final Report Quality Standard

## Purpose

This standard governs every customer-facing Resourceful property report. The objective is not to manufacture a long PDF. The objective is to produce a branded, comprehensive, accurate, evidence-led work product whose depth is proportionate to the assignment and whose reasoning can be followed, challenged, and verified.

A professional report earns its length through documented evidence, analysis, exhibits, and limitations. Unsupported filler, repeated boilerplate, fabricated precision, and decorative page count are prohibited.

The internal benchmark that informed this standard is a completed commercial property-tax appeal appraisal. It is a structural and quality reference only. Resourceful must not copy another firm's branding, proprietary language, certification, signature, credentials, or work product.

## Report identity and professional boundary

Every report must identify the exact product being delivered:

- **Resourceful analysis:** an AI-assisted evidence synthesis and valuation workfile generated from documented inputs.
- **Licensed appraisal:** only when an identified licensed appraiser has reviewed, adopted, certified, and signed the report under the applicable professional and jurisdictional requirements.
- **Filing support:** preparation and guidance are not represented as legal representation, filing completion, deadline verification, or guaranteed acceptance unless separately confirmed by an authorized person and documented system of record.

A Resourceful report may be rigorous, detailed, and persuasive without falsely representing itself as a signed appraisal, engineering report, survey, inspection, legal opinion, or tax-representative engagement.

## Required report sequence

Sections are conditional on assignment type and available evidence, but the report should follow this order when applicable:

1. **Letter of transmittal**
   - Client and property identification.
   - Assignment purpose and effective valuation date.
   - Concise property summary.
   - Concluded value or result, subject to stated limitations.
   - Clear identity of the report preparer and professional boundary.

2. **Branded cover**
   - Resourceful identity and consistent design system.
   - Assignment-specific report title.
   - Subject image when available.
   - Full address, jurisdiction, parcel number, valuation date, and report date.

3. **Dynamic table of contents and evidence inventory**
   - Include only sections actually rendered.
   - State counts for comparable sales, rental evidence, subject photographs, and maps.
   - Distinguish certification from general use disclosures.

4. **Summary of salient facts**
   - Property identification, rights or interest analyzed, ownership and parcel data.
   - Site and improvement facts.
   - Assessment facts and assessor-implied market value where an assessment level applies.
   - Highest and best use conclusions when supported.
   - Approach indications, concluded value, value per unit, valuation premise, and effective date.
   - Explicitly label unavailable or unapplied approaches.

5. **Subject maps and visual evidence**
   - Regional map.
   - Neighborhood map.
   - Parcel or tax map when available.
   - Full subject photograph exhibit, not a four-image sample.
   - Floor plan, site plan, aerial, or other diagrams when supplied and source-qualified.
   - Captions and source limitations for every exhibit.

6. **Assignment, intended use, and scope of work**
   - Intended user and intended use.
   - Assignment type and valuation question.
   - Effective date and property interest analyzed.
   - Data researched and analyses performed.
   - Inspection status: no inspection, owner-supplied imagery, third-party inspection, or licensed appraisal inspection.
   - Extraordinary assumptions, hypothetical conditions, and known limitations when applicable.
   - Explicit list of procedures not performed.

7. **Subject property and jurisdiction evidence**
   - Property description and use.
   - Site and improvement descriptions.
   - Property history and recent transfers.
   - Assessment history and current assessment.
   - Zoning and permitted-use evidence.
   - Flood, parcel, access, legal-description, or environmental information only when sourced and appropriately limited.

8. **Area and market analysis**
   - Relevant metropolitan, county, city, and neighborhood context.
   - Property-type-specific market conditions.
   - Vacancy, rent, sale-volume, pricing, supply, demand, and trend evidence when available.
   - Every statistic must have a source and relevant period.
   - Broad market commentary must connect to the subject and valuation date.

9. **Highest and best use analysis**
   - Legally permissible.
   - Physically possible.
   - Financially feasible.
   - Maximally productive.
   - Separate conclusions as vacant and as improved when the evidence supports both.
   - Do not auto-generate a definitive conclusion from incomplete zoning, site, or market evidence.

10. **Approaches to value**
    - Sales comparison, income capitalization, and cost approach are included only when supported.
    - Each approach explains its method, evidence, calculations, limitations, and conclusion.
    - Omitted approaches state why they were not applied.

11. **Reconciliation and value conclusion**
    - Summarize each supported approach and its role.
    - Explain weighting or reliance rather than merely averaging indications.
    - Reconcile arithmetic and material differences.
    - State the Resourceful concluded market value and effective date.
    - Preserve the licensed-appraisal boundary.

12. **Assignment-specific action guide**
    - Tax appeal filing instructions.
    - Pre-purchase negotiation guide.
    - Pre-listing pricing strategy.
    - Independent valuation use and next-step guidance.
    - Jurisdiction facts override generated prose.

13. **Limitations, disclosures, and certification**
    - General assumptions and limiting conditions.
    - Data-source and reliance limitations.
    - AI-assisted preparation disclosure.
    - No-inspection and no-survey boundaries.
    - Licensed certification, signature, license number, expiration, and qualifications only when genuinely applicable.

## Evidence standards by approach

### Sales comparison approach

A supported sales comparison section should include:

- Preferably three or more closed sales; five or more for complex commercial assignments when credible evidence is available.
- Property address, jurisdiction, transaction date, sale price, property type, site area, building area, age, use, and unit price.
- Source URL or licensed source identifier and retrieval date.
- Closed-sale status; listings and offerings must be separately identified.
- Distance and geographic relationship to the subject.
- Transaction adjustments: property rights, financing, conditions of sale, and market conditions.
- Property adjustments: location, size, site-to-building ratio, age, condition, utility, quality, office percentage, clear height, parking, loading, tenancy, or other relevant characteristics.
- Adjustment method and source support.
- Unadjusted and adjusted ranges, central tendency, and subject conclusion.
- Individual comparable profile pages for material commercial assignments.
- A comparable map when available.

No percentage adjustment may appear as an unexplained fact. Each adjustment must be traceable to cited evidence, a disclosed calculation, or a documented qualitative judgment. Automated photo observations may inform review notes but must not automatically write dollar or percentage adjustments into the valuation.

### Income capitalization approach

A supported income section should include:

- Comparable rental survey with source, date, status, building or leased area, lease type, rent, and effective net rent.
- Clear distinction between listing rent, asking rent, executed lease, contract rent, and effective rent.
- Market-rent conclusion and rationale.
- Potential gross income.
- Other income, if supported.
- Vacancy and collection loss with market support.
- Effective gross income.
- Operating expenses with source or disclosed ratio method.
- Net operating income.
- Capitalization-rate range and source.
- Concluded capitalization rate and rationale.
- Direct-capitalization arithmetic: NOI divided by cap rate.
- Stored indication compared with recomputed arithmetic and material variance disclosure.
- Value per square foot or other relevant unit.

A completed income indication requires positive finite NOI, a valid decimal capitalization rate greater than zero and no more than one, and a positive stored conclusion. Missing values must not be replaced with zero. A cap rate entered as `8` instead of `0.08` is invalid.

### Cost approach

A supported cost section should include:

- Building area and source.
- Replacement or reproduction cost input and source.
- Implied cost per unit.
- Physical depreciation and method.
- Functional and external obsolescence when applicable.
- Effective age and economic life when supported.
- Depreciated improvement indication.
- Land value and source.
- Stored indication compared with recomputed arithmetic.
- Disclosure of missing land or unexplained reconciliation inputs.

A positive stored total alone does not constitute a complete cost approach.

## Assessment and appeal standards

For property-tax assignments:

- Distinguish assessed value, assessment level, equalized assessed value, assessor-implied market value, and tax liability.
- Normalize fractional assessment levels before comparing assessment to market value.
- Never compare a raw fractional assessed value directly with a full market-value conclusion.
- Do not claim annual tax savings without a verified tax rate, applicable equalization factors, exemptions, and a supported post-appeal assessment.
- Label legacy reduction fields accurately as assessment reduction rather than tax savings.
- Include jurisdiction, township, parcel number, filing authority, deadline status, required documents, form, fee, hearing format, and verified filing URL when known.
- Generated deadlines, fees, forms, URLs, or representation authority must never override verified jurisdiction records.
- A winning outcome requires a verifiable new assessed value below the original assessment.

## Visual and branding standards

- Use the Resourceful PDF design tokens, typography, spacing, colors, headers, footers, and page numbering consistently.
- Maintain a restrained professional identity rather than imitating another appraisal firm's visual system.
- Use high-resolution images with legible captions.
- Keep maps and diagrams at readable scale.
- Avoid orphaned headings, split calculation rows, clipped tables, repeated blank pages, overlapping content, and unreadably small text.
- Use deliberate page breaks for major sections and individual commercial comparable profiles.
- Tables must distinguish subject, comparable, source, stored input, calculated output, and unavailable data.
- Use `Unverified`, `Not available`, or `Not applied` instead of a dash where omission could be mistaken for a zero.
- Do not use decorative charts unless they communicate sourced evidence.

## Length guidance

Page count is an outcome, not a target. The following ranges are planning guidance, not release gates:

- Residential tax appeal: approximately 20–40 pages.
- Commercial or industrial tax appeal: approximately 35–70+ pages.
- Independent valuation analysis: approximately 20–50 pages.
- Pre-purchase or pre-listing analysis: approximately 15–40 pages.

A complex property with maps, photographs, five sales, rental evidence, market analysis, and filing guidance may properly exceed these ranges. A simpler property should not be padded to reach them.

## Release gates

A final report must not be delivered when any applicable hard failure exists:

- Missing or zero concluded value.
- No evidence-backed valuation approach.
- Unsupported zero-comparable conclusion without a complete reconciled income or cost approach.
- Material arithmetic mismatch not explained in the report.
- Tax appeal report missing appeal reasoning or filing guidance.
- Invalid or unverified generated filing link presented as authoritative.
- Unresolved synthetic or fabricated transaction evidence.
- Required customer or licensed review not completed.
- Report title, certification, or signature implies credentials that are not present.

Warnings may permit delivery only when they are visible, understandable, and do not undermine the core conclusion.

## Quality assurance

Every release candidate should be validated through:

1. Lint and type-check.
2. Unit and policy tests.
3. Production build.
4. Representative residential and commercial PDF generation.
5. Page-by-page visual review at Letter size.
6. Checks for clipping, overflow, blank pages, broken images, broken links, inconsistent totals, and stale branding.
7. Arithmetic reconciliation of every displayed valuation approach.
8. Verification that the table of contents matches rendered sections.
9. Verification that all customer-facing factual claims are sourced or explicitly qualified.
10. Review of the final report by a qualified human when the assignment, jurisdiction, customer promise, or professional designation requires it.

## Governance

- This standard is version-controlled and applies to all report renderers.
- New report sections require a source policy, failure behavior, and regression test.
- Provider or model changes may not weaken evidence requirements.
- Report quality is measured by credibility, traceability, decision usefulness, and visual integrity—not by generated word count.
